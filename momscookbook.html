06 · UX Research, Editorial & Illustrations

Mom's Recipe

A living archive of maternal culinary warmth — preserving not just dishes, but memories, stories, and love.

Born from a classroom observation of algorithmic food culture: 15-second fast-cooking reels and trend diets offer speed, but lack emotional grounding. For young Indians living away from home, true nourishment is the familiar comfort of maa ke haath ka khana.

Role Product Design, Research & Illustrations
Timeline 4 Weeks · Solo Project
Core Audience Indian Youth (18–35)
Output Memory Zine & Digital Website
01 · Background & Problem

Familiarity over novelty: What truly nourishes us?

This project originated from a classroom exercise analyzing Instagram algorithms and content trends. While studying the surge in self-care meal prep and 15-minute quick-meal reels, a glaring void emerged: these formats were practical, yet devoid of emotional resonance or cultural grounding — especially for youth living away from their hometowns.

Home food is rarely about rigid grams or timers. It is an intuitive sensory language: muscle memory, aromatic cues, and the emotional safety that only a mother's kitchen embodies. Yet when students and young professionals attempt to cook these dishes alone, recipes transmitted via frantic phone calls and messy WhatsApp chats often end in frustration.

02 · User Research & Insights

Listening to young diaspora & students

To understand the behavioural dynamics of cooking and memory, I conducted structured and unstructured interviews alongside a detailed quantitative survey with 20 young Indians aged 18–35 living in hostels, rented flats, and working in cities across Delhi, Bangalore, Mumbai, and abroad.

88% Emotional Solace

Directly associate home food with psychological comfort, warmth, and feeling safe.

65% Delivery Reliance

Rely on Swiggy or Zomato as their primary meal source, forced to prioritize convenience over health.

68.4% Family Sources

Get recipes directly from mothers or grandmothers, with 58% supplementing via YouTube.

29% The Intuition Barrier

Repeatedly fail to replicate the taste because maternal cooking relies on intuition ("swaad anusar").

What the Community Needed

Participants articulated clear friction points in current digital culinary tools, expressing demand for tactile and sensory guidance:

82.4% Visual Hand Techniques

Short, focused clips showing the exact sizzle of tadka and dough texture rather than generic studio shots.

70.6% "Feeling Homesick?" Filter

Mood-first dish categorization tailored to exhaustion, nostalgia, and emotional recovery.

65.0% Maternal Audio Notes

Voice notes from mothers explaining sensory benchmarks and offering warm reassurance.

User Personas

Ananya, 21 College Student · Hostel Living

“I go home just to eat maa ke haath ka khaana.”

  • Pain Points: Craves home food, cannot cook well, feels homesick. Overwhelmed by study routines; defaults to Maggi or food delivery apps.
  • Habits: Watches food reels on Instagram and Pinterest; wishes cooking felt less clinical.
  • Goals: Learn forgiving, basic comfort meals; feel more grounded in a small hostel room.
Shriyansi, 28 Working Professional · Metro City

“The torai sabzi my mom makes is different and I like it that way only.”

  • Pain Points: Busy corporate week, less time to cook, convenience overtaking nutrition and mindful eating.
  • Habits: Does weekly grocery runs, values wellness, but struggles to translate mom's oral phone advice into reality.
  • Goals: Weekend home cooking; trying heirloom family recipes without feeling unsure of herself.

Food as an Emotional Memory Artifact

Across participant conversations, food emerged as an anchor for family history and childhood safety:

Palyor (Pahadi Dish)

“My father declared he would cook for the first time. Watching him take over the kitchen with joy made that simple dish an indelible memory.”

Sunday Rajma Chawal

“It was our ritual every Sunday after a grueling week. I wish I had written down her exact rajma recipe before moving away.”

Toasted Aloo Sandwich

“When I told mom I liked it after coming home from school, she made it for me every single week with love.”

03 · Solution Architecture

Preserving heritage in two complementary mediums

To balance tangible physical intimacy with accessible digital reach, the project bifurcated into two complementary formats:

Tactile Artifact

The Printed Keepsake Zine

A physical cookbook celebrating maternal culinary artifacts, original hand-drawn ingredient illustrations, family photographs, and warm handwritten instructions.

  • Sensory, screen-free tactile interaction in the kitchen.
  • Archived personal stories and generational cooking quirks.
  • Interactive digital flip book experience for remote readers.
Digital Platform

The Community Recipe Website

A digital recipe sanctuary connecting young cooks with maternal wisdom, voice notes, and emotional categories that translate oral intuition into accessible steps.

  • Mood-first navigation: "Feeling Homesick?", "Midnight Comfort".
  • Maa ki audio clips explaining sensory aromas and cooking cues.
  • Shared community kitchen where families preserve heirloom dishes.
