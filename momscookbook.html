03 · UX Research & Editorial Design

Mom's Recipe

A living archive of maternal culinary warmth — preserving not just dishes, but memories, stories, and love.

Born from a classroom observation of algorithmic food culture: 15-second fast-cooking reels and trend diets offer speed, but lack emotional grounding. For young Indians living away from home, true nourishment is the familiar comfort of maa ke haath ka khana.

Role Product Design & Research
Timeline 4 Weeks · Solo Project
Core Audience Indian Youth (18–35)
Output Memory Zine & Digital App
01 · Background & Problem

Familiarity over novelty: What truly nourishes us?

In a world dominated by instant delivery apps and rapid-fire recipe reels, cooking has become hyper-transactional. We are inundated with high-protein hacks and exotic trends, yet youth away from home report feeling chronic culinary isolation.

Home food is rarely about rigid grams or temperatures — it is about intuition, muscle memory, and the emotional sanctuary that only a mother's kitchen provides. Yet when students and young professionals attempt to cook these dishes alone, recipes transmitted via patchy phone calls and chaotic WhatsApp chats end in frustration.

02 · User Research & Insights

Listening to young diaspora & students

I conducted surveys and qualitative interviews with young Indians aged 18–35 living in hostels, shared flats, and abroad. The numbers revealed a stark gap between the desire for maternal cooking and the ability to replicate it:

85% Emotional Comfort

Associate immediate psychological warmth and solace with home food over restaurant dining.

68.4% Family Transmitted

Rely solely on mothers or grandmothers for recipes rather than cookbooks or culinary blogs.

29% Replication Gap

Repeatedly fail to recreate the authentic taste due to vague intuitive measures like "swaad anusar" and "thoda sa".

When asked what would bridge this generational and geographic disconnect, users highlighted three non-negotiable features:

82.4% Visual Hand Techniques

Short, focused clips showing the exact color of roasted tadka and dough consistency.

70.6% "Feeling Homesick?" Filter

An intuitive emotion-first filter serving recipes tailored to sadness, exhaustion, or nostalgia.

65.0% Maternal Audio Notes

Short voice snippets from mothers sharing affectionate reminders and step-by-step encouragement.

03 · Solution Architecture

Preserving heritage in two complementary mediums

Rather than building a standard utilitarian recipe database, the project bifurcated into two empathetic formats designed to capture both the tangible nostalgia and the daily utility of maternal recipes:

Tactile Artifact

The Printed Memory Zine

A physical keepsake cookbook celebrating maternal culinary artifacts, hand-drawn illustrations, family photographs, and warm handwritten marginalia.

  • Sensory, screen-free tactile interaction in the kitchen.
  • Archived personal stories and generational cooking wisdom.
  • Interactive digital flip book experience for remote readers.
Digital Platform

The Community Recipe Sanctuary

A mobile platform connecting young home cooks with maternal wisdom, voice notes, and contextual tips that translate intuition into accessible steps.

  • Mood-first navigation: "Feeling Homesick?", "Midnight Comfort".
  • Maa ki audio clips explaining sensory cues.
  • Crowdsourced family archives preserved for future generations.
04 · Community Feedback

Validation through authentic resonance

Testing both the physical zine proofs and the digital prototype with college students produced deeply emotional reactions:
