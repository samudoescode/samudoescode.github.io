01 — Overview

BookNest

Where readers hatch new habits
UX Research Product Design November 2025 12 Weeks 65 surveys · 24 interviews

BookNest is a reading platform concept that started with one research question: why have digitally connected generations stopped reading? The answer wasn't literacy — it was discovery, motivation, and the absence of genuine community.

How might we
Design a digital experience that makes reading feel emotionally rewarding, socially supported, and easy to start and sustain — rather than overwhelming, isolating, or pressurised?
02 — The Problem

Not a literacy crisis.
A discovery crisis.

The average human attention span today sits at 47 seconds. Dopamine-driven platforms have rewired how we engage with content — making focused reading feel effortful, slow, and solitary by comparison. The problem isn't that people can't read. It's that they can't start.

📱
Distraction
Short-form content (Reels, TikTok) has colonised the reading hour without users consciously choosing it.
🧠
Decision paralysis
"Top-10 lists stress me out." Too much choice, no emotional filter — leading to not choosing at all.
😮‍💨
Exhaustion
Mental fatigue from screen time and work makes starting a long book feel like another obligation, not an escape.
03 — Research

What the data revealed

Surveys with 65 respondents and in-depth interviews with 24 participants across college students, young professionals, and returning readers. The consistent theme: users don't lack desire — they lack a gentle, structured path back to reading.

85%
chose books based on friends' recommendations — not algorithms or bestseller lists
80%
needed better taste-matched discovery — genre labels alone weren't doing the job
70%
believed community would help them sustain reading — they just didn't have the right kind
68%
cited lack of time as their barrier — but interviews revealed this was really a lack of system
60%
wanted gentle everyday reminders — not competitive pressure or gamified leaderboards
50%
were open to AI snippets to help commit — a quick preview before investing in a whole book
Recurring qualitative theme · 24 interviews
"Reading should feel relaxing — not like another task or competition."
Users consistently rejected high-pressure gamification. They wanted gentle accountability, not leaderboards. This directly shaped the design of the streaks feature.
04 — Market Gap

Where existing platforms fall short

A cross-analysis of seven platforms (Goodreads, StoryGraph, TBR, Likewise, BookyCall, Bookfinity, WhatShouldIReadNext) revealed a consistent gap: apps focus on organising or tracking books, not on helping users find the emotional entry point that makes them actually open one.

Dimension Goodreads AO3 (reference) BookNest
Discovery Static lists, trend-driven, not context-aware Deep trope + emotional tagging ("slow burn", "hurt/comfort") Mood, trope & time-aware — hybrid AI + human taxonomy
Motivation None — metrics only, no nudges Fandom culture drives participation Gentle streaks, soft nudges — no competitive pressure
Community Limited discussions, dated UI Strong fandom but niche and siloed Small interest-based clubs, shared progress loops
Reviews Overwhelming, spoiler-heavy, unmoderated Emotional commentary via tags Spoiler-safe, layered, emotionally tagged feedback
UX design Outdated — represents design stagnation Functional, not designed for newcomers Calm, minimal, dopamine-neutral environment

AO3 was the most instructive reference — not as a competitor but as proof that emotional, trope-based discovery systems work at scale and drive engagement. BookNest borrows the taxonomy model and applies it to long-form reading.

06 — Insights → Design

How research shaped the product

Each of the three core features maps directly to a validated research finding. Nothing was assumed — everything was discovered.

Research finding
85% discover books through people they trust, not algorithms. Interviews confirmed users browse by emotional resonance and feeling — not by genre. AO3's trope taxonomy proved this model works at scale.
Design decision
Trope Finder
Browse by mood, emotional theme, and trope ("Found Family", "Slow Burn") instead of genre dropdowns. Negative filters let users exclude what they don't want — equally important for reducing decision fatigue.
cozy & slow found family no horror
Research finding
60% wanted reminders; interviews unanimously rejected leaderboards. Users wanted accountability without anxiety — gentle encouragement that rewards showing up, not competing with others.
Design decision
Reading Streaks
A soft, visual accountability system — encouraging, not competitive. Daily reading check-ins tracked as personal streaks. Progress stays private. Missing one day doesn't feel catastrophic.
Research finding
70% said community would sustain their habits — but big forums and passive Goodreads shelves feel impersonal. Users wanted small, real conversations around shared reads, not a feed.
Design decision
Micro-Clubs
Small, user-created book clubs around shared interests or ongoing reads. Focused discussion, shared progress bars, peer discovery — turning reading from a solitary act into a collaborative one.
07 — The Screens

Key moments in the experience

Built on a calm, dark visual system — deep purples, warm amber accents, soft greens. Designed to feel like a reading environment rather than another app demanding your attention.

08 — Refinements

What changed after testing

Usability testing across the core navigation flow (Home → Discover → Book Detail) surfaced recurring friction points. The refined screens make motivation visible earlier and reduce the cognitive effort of choosing a book.

⚠ Before
·Streak and motivation elements were buried — users missed them entirely
·Category labels were genre-only — lacked the emotional nuance users actually browse by
·AI summary was hidden deep in the book detail screen
·Actions (shelf, AI, tags) weren't visually distinct — confused first-time users
✓ After
→Streak moved to the home screen — the first thing users see on open
→Mood-first discovery cards replace genre dropdowns throughout
→AI snippet surfaced earlier — appears on discovery cards, before committing
→Clearer labelling, stronger visual hierarchy and action affordances
09 — Outcome

What BookNest proposes

BookNest treats reading engagement as a design problem, not a motivation problem. Research confirmed that users haven't lost the desire to read — their attention has adapted to environments that optimise for instant reward.

Effective UX design can rebuild long-form attention by creating restorative, emotionally intelligent spaces. Each of the three features — emotional discovery, gentle gamification, micro-communities — addresses a specific research-validated barrier rather than layering features for their own sake.

Future scope
AI-driven mood recommendations trained on reading history and time-of-day context
Future scope
Audiobook tracking integration to broaden habit-building beyond text reading
Future scope
Author + creator ecosystem for Q&As, events, and micro-content within clubs
