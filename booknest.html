Case Study · UX Research · 2025

BookNest

Where readers hatch new habits.

A reading platform concept that started with one research question: why have digitally connected generations stopped reading? The answer wasn't literacy — it was discovery, motivation, and the absence of genuine community.

RoleUX Research · Product Design
Duration12 weeks · Nov 2025
TeamSolo project
Research65 surveys · 24 interviews
ToolsFigma · Notion · Dovetail
01 · Overview

BookNest reframes reading engagement as a design problem, not a motivation one. Across twelve weeks of mixed-methods research, the consistent theme wasn't that users had stopped wanting to read — it was that the path back to reading had quietly disappeared, replaced by environments designed to capture attention rather than restore it.

The product is a calm, dark-mode reading companion built around three research-validated features — emotional discovery, gentle gamification, and micro-communities.

Design a digital experience that makes reading feel emotionally rewarding, socially supported, and easy to start and sustain — rather than overwhelming, isolating, or pressurised?
02 · The Problem

Not a literacy crisis.
A discovery crisis.

The average human attention span today sits at 47 seconds. Dopamine-driven platforms have rewired how we engage with content — making focused reading feel effortful, slow, and solitary by comparison. The problem isn't that people can't read. It's that they can't start.

📱

Distraction

Short-form content (Reels, TikTok) has colonised the reading hour without users consciously choosing it.

🧠

Decision paralysis

"Top-10 lists stress me out." Too much choice, no emotional filter — leading to not choosing at all.

😮‍💨

Exhaustion

Mental fatigue from screen time and work makes starting a long book feel like another obligation, not an escape.

03 · Research

What the data revealed.

Surveys with 65 respondents and in-depth interviews with 24 participants across college students, young professionals, and returning readers. The consistent theme: users don't lack desire — they lack a gentle, structured path back to reading.

85%picked books through recommendations from friends and family — not algorithms or bestseller lists
80%needed books better suited to their taste — genre labels alone weren't doing the job
70%said community would help them keep reading — they just didn't have the right kind
60%wanted gentle everyday reminders — not competitive pressure or leaderboards
Reading should feel relaxing — not like another task or competition. Recurring qualitative theme · 24 interviews

Users consistently rejected high-pressure gamification. They wanted gentle accountability, not leaderboards. This directly shaped the design of the streaks feature.

05 · Personas

Who we're designing for.

Target audience: digitally-native readers aged 18–32 who have either never built a consistent reading habit or have lost one to short-form content. They want to read — they open Goodreads, they follow BookTok — but they don't finish books. The barrier is always the same: too much friction at the start, too little support to continue.

Two core users distilled from the research — one at the beginning of a reading habit, one trying to rebuild one they've lost.

06 · Insights → Design

How research shaped the product.

Each of the three core features maps directly to a validated research finding. Nothing was assumed — everything was discovered.

Research finding

85% discover books through people they trust, not algorithms. Interviews confirmed users browse by emotional resonance and feeling — not by genre. AO3's trope taxonomy proved this model works at scale.

Design decision

Trope Finder

Browse by mood, emotional theme, and trope ("Found Family", "Slow Burn") instead of genre dropdowns. Negative filters let users exclude what they don't want — equally important for reducing decision fatigue.

cozy & slow found family no horror
Research finding

60% wanted reminders; interviews unanimously rejected leaderboards. Users wanted accountability without anxiety — gentle encouragement that rewards showing up, not competing with others.

Design decision

Reading Streaks

A soft, visual accountability system — encouraging, not competitive. Daily reading check-ins tracked as personal streaks. Progress stays private. Missing one day doesn't feel catastrophic.

Research finding

70% said community would sustain their habits — but big forums and passive Goodreads shelves feel impersonal. Users wanted small, real conversations around shared reads, not a feed.

Design decision

Micro-Clubs

Small, user-created book clubs around shared interests or ongoing reads. Focused discussion, shared progress bars, peer discovery — turning reading from a solitary act into a collaborative one.

07 · The Screens

Key moments in the experience.

Built on a calm, dark visual system — deep purples, warm amber accents, soft greens. Designed to feel like a reading environment rather than another app demanding your attention.

Hover a phone to see the research finding behind each design choice. The screens follow the site theme — toggle via the title bar to see them in light mode.

AO3 as a design reference

AO3 (Archive of Our Own) proved at scale that readers navigate by emotional resonance — "Slow Burn", "Found Family", "Enemies to Lovers" — not genre buckets. 85% of survey respondents picked books by personal recommendation and feeling, not bestseller rank. The Trope Finder directly borrows AO3's taxonomy model and applies it to published fiction, with negative filters ("no horror", "no cliffhangers") equally important for reducing decision fatigue. This is one of the three core features of BookNest, and the one most directly validated by both the research and an existing system that already works.

BookNest · Brand
The app icon abstracts the BookNest name — purple sun rising over a woven nest of pages. The mascot, Tota, is a recurring character used on empty states and streak rewards. Both are intentionally hand-drawn to keep the product feeling warm and lo-fi, not clinical.
08 · Outcome

Conclusion & future scope.

Audiences haven't lost the desire to read — their attention has simply adapted to seek emotional connection and low-friction discovery. Effective UX, as demonstrated by the BookNest concept, can create restorative digital spaces that nurture long-form attention.

Future scope

AI-driven mood and "vibe" recommendations could be further investigated — trained on reading history, time-of-day, and emotional context.

Future scope

Audiobook tracking to broaden habit-building beyond text-only reading, integrated into the same streak system.
